<summary>Learning Outcomes</summary>
<div class="accordion-content">
<p>After passing this course successfully students are able to ...</p>
<ul>
  <li>create, compile and run a project in an IDE.</li>
  <li>create formatted output of textual and numeric data using format specifiers.</li>
  <li>apply arithmetic operators to perform calculations using variables.</li>
  <li>read and store textual and numeric values, perform numeric calculations and print the result.</li>

<summary>A few remarks and tips</summary>
<div class="accordion-content">
<p>Programming is a great skill since you are capable of actually building something. You do not build physical artifacts like carpenters or blacksmiths do, instead you build smart, clever, efficient, elegant and powerful artifacts. It is rewarding to have created a working piece of software.</p>

<p>However, learning how to program is a time consuming task, similar to the more traditional crafts. You have to start with small projects and really understand everything before you can move on to mastering the next stage.</p>

<p>Since a program is expressed in a (programming) language it is not surprising that the learning process is similar to that of learning a natural language. When learning a foreign (natural) language you have to learn vocabulary and grammar. You learn common phrases and soon you start to understand what others have written or spoken and building on this you start expressing yourself, your thoughts and ideas. You start with simple things (asking the way, what are your hobbies, ...) before you discuss more complex things like the ontological status of the electron in the universe.</p>

<p>A similar statement is true for programming. There is vocabulary to know and grammar to understand. There are phrases to learn and build upon. You have to be able to read and understand what others have written and eventually you will be able to express your own ideas, the ideas of how to solve problems. Of course there are simpler problems to start with and more complex problems that often build on solutions of the simpler problems. It also means that you need to learn two things: 1. how to find solutions to problems and 2. how to express those solution in a programming language.</p>

<p>This course introduces you to a large number of fundamental concepts and forms the base of your programming education and training in this study program. They will be required in many of the courses of the upcoming semesters as well as in a professional IT environment.</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<p><strong>From the above we can extract a few tips:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>theory is necessary - <strong>prepare for the classes!</strong></li>
  <li>theory is not sufficient, programming needs practice - <strong>do the exercises and do them **yourself**!</strong></li>
  <li>it is necessary to understand the previous topic before you move on to the next - <strong>do not skip things, rather invest time to review!</strong></li>
  <li>before you can write down your idea (how to solve a problem) you have to have this idea in the first place - <strong>think about how to solve a problem before you start coding!</strong></li>
</ul>
</div>

<details>
<summary>Getting help</summary>
<div class="accordion-content">
<p>Sometimes you can get stuck doing programming exercises and then it is time to get help. When getting help from other students or friends</p>
<ul>
  <li>ask for a hint/advice/help and <strong>not for correct code/solution</strong>. You will have to be able to program yourself!</li>
  <li>let them know your programming skill level, so they can give you suitable and useful advice on your level. Often advanced students are tempted to present advanced solutions that confuse and/or frustrate you.</li>
</ul>

<p>You are encouraged to use the course's forum, however when posting a question keep the following in mind:</p>
<ul>
  <li>State what you are working on (e.g. which Exercise)</li>
  <li>Describe the steps you perform(ed)</li>
  <li>Describe the <strong>expected</strong> behavior or outcome</li>
  <li>Describe the <strong>actual</strong> behavior or outcome</li>
  <li>Describe everything you have tried so far to resolve the problem (and which causes you can therefore rule out).</li>
  <li>Add <strong>relevant</strong> code-snippets and/or screenshots</li>
  <li>Ask for advice/help/explanation</li>
</ul>
<p>These information will help reproduce and eventually solve the problem.</p>

<summary>About pen and paper on Exams</summary>
<div class="accordion-content">
<p>We say it loud and clear: "Some exams will be programming with <strong>pen and paper</strong>!"</p>

<p>Are we serious? Yes, definitely, and here is why:</p>

<p>Computers, internet and AI are fascinating and of great help for developers out in the industry. When you try to solve a tricky programming problem you ask google and in a split second you find a solution, copy and paste it, and ... done! In modern IDEs you type a line of code and the AI-backed codepilot or a similar solution suggests a complete program.</p>

<p>All the problems we will do in this course have been solved millions of times and the solutions are all over the internet, some of the tasks even have a thread on stackoverflow and the codepilots sure know them all. Finding the solution on the internet, however, is not the same as finding a solution yourself. And eventually you will have to solve new problems that have not been solved yet (read an interesting, recent post about "<a href="https://pvs-studio.com/en/blog/posts/0952/" target="_blank">how one idiot hired a couple more idiots</a>").</p>

<p>That is why you will have to solve a (new) problem without the help of the internet or the IDE (or others) with pen and paper at midterm.</p>

<p>In the analogy of learning a natural, foreign language like English or French: When you speak the language you can of course look up technical, specialist terms when you need them, but you have to know the basics. You can not afford to look up all the words of the basic "what are your hobbies?" dialogue or the 1st grade "grocery shopping dialogue". You will not get anything done in reasonable time and it is probably wrong to say that you know the language. You just know how to look up words.</p>

<p>This course is only slightly above the "hobbies" or "grocery" dialogue level. You can <strong>NOT</strong> afford to look anything up - you just have to know!</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<p><strong>So, in summary:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>(in the beginning) no IDEs because they are too powerful</li>
  <li>no internet because you have to find a solution yourself</li>
  <li>no books or notes because you have to know the basics</li>
</ul>
</div>

<details>
<summary>On AI</summary>
<div class="accordion-content">
<p>ChatGPT, PerplexityAI, Co-Pilot et al are great tools to speed up many tasks. All of the tasks and assignments (or similar incarnations thereof) in this course have been completed countless times in universities and schools around the globe for decades and the aforementioned tools can probably solve all of them and pass the course.</p>

<p>To put it another way, the tools are great to fill in right answers (most of the time at least), which is exactly why you do not earn any points for filling in right answers at home. You only earn points for answers you come up with yourself at tests or exams - without internet or AI.</p>

<p>The learning outcome of this course is that you are able to write programs to solve problems, because we believe it is necessary to learn that before you solve new problems (that the AI can maybe not solve). AI can help you reach that goal, but not by producing solutions you cannot come up with yourself.</p>
